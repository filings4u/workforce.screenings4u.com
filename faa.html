THE FAA REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

Safety-sensitive roles. Documented programs. Clear responsibilities.

FAA workplace drug and alcohol testing is governed by 14 CFR Part 120, with DOT testing procedures under 49 CFR Part 40. Requirements depend on the employer, operation, and duties performed. View FAA regulations →

WHO MAY BE COVERED

FAA testing requirements depend on your operation.

FAA Part 120 applies to specified air carriers, air tour operators, non-FAA air traffic control facilities, and certain employers or contractors performing safety-sensitive functions. The specific program establishment process differs by operating authority.

✓

Part 121 and 135 operators — relevant operations specifications and program duties

✓

Part 91.147 air tour operators — applicable letter of authorization and testing obligations

✓

Other covered facilities and contractors — registration or alternative arrangements as applicable

A NOTE FOR AVIATION C/TPAS

Employer responsibility doesn't disappear when services are outsourced.

Employers remain responsible for complying with applicable FAA and DOT rules even when using service agents. For random pool administration, C/TPAs must verify that people in a DOT-regulated pool are actually covered by a relevant DOT testing program. The FAA does not approve employer or C/TPA testing programs.
