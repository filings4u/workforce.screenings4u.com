US COAST GUARD CHEMICAL TESTING

Regulated crews require organized testing programs.

46 CFR Part 16 sets out required chemical testing programs for covered marine employers and crewmembers, including random drug testing. 46 CFR Part 4, Subpart 4.06 establishes separate testing duties following serious marine incidents. Applicable procedures also involve 49 CFR Part 40.

View USCG chemical-testing regulations →
KNOW YOUR COVERED CREW

Coverage follows the role and vessel operation.

Depending on the inspected or uninspected vessel and duties performed, chemical-testing rules can cover crewmembers in the following roles.

01

Credentialed mariners

Captains, operators and other personnel required to hold a Coast Guard credential for assigned duties.

02

Safe vessel operations

Crew performing functions directly related to safe vessel operation, when covered by the rule.

03

Watch & security duties

Personnel assigned certain required patrol, watch or emergency-response duties.

04

Covered vessel crews

Other crewmembers covered under the applicable inspected-vessel and uninspected-vessel provisions.

WORKFORCE / RANDOM SELECTIONS
SERIOUS MARINE INCIDENTS

Know the time-sensitive testing rules.

When an event qualifies as a serious marine incident, marine employers must make a prompt determination and arrange required testing for directly involved individuals. Safety-related exceptions and documentation duties may apply.

2 hours

Alcohol testing target

Alcohol testing must generally be conducted within two hours following the incident, unless safety concerns prevent it. Testing is not required after eight hours.

32 hours

Drug specimen collection

Drug specimen collection must generally occur within 32 hours of the incident, subject to the regulation's safety-related exception.

Reference: U.S. Coast Guard serious marine incident testing guidance.
